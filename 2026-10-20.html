Koel calls twice in Tuesday's window

Two calls from a summer migrant, 370 calls in all and ten species across the 24 hours to 9:30 pm in Canberra.

The Pacific Koel gave two calls in the window, the first at 5:18 pm and the last at 7:37 pm. It has been heard on 5 of the 62 days the station has been listening. The species is a summer migrant to the Canberra region, most often recorded in January, November and December, and is a moderately common bird around Canberra.

The timing stands out: this was an evening voice in a day otherwise carried by the dawn. The koel is numbered among the later spring arrivals, reaching Canberra in October and November as the migrants return from the north.

Its scientific name is Eudynamys orientalis. Unlike most parasitic cuckoos, Pacific Koel chicks do not try to kill the host's own chicks.

The Regular

Australian Magpie

The Australian Magpie was heard on 60 of the 62 days the station has been listening, a presence kept up again today with 16 calls. Its first call came at 5:29 am and its last at 10:57 am, and it opened the dawn chorus at 5:48 am, 27 minutes before sunrise.

Pairs of magpies sing a loud musical duet called carolling to advertise or defend their territory. The species lives in the Canberra region year-round and is one of the most widespread birds around Canberra.
