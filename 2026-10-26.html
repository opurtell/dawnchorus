Common myna arrives on the station's list

A bird the station has never recorded before gave two calls this morning, one of 13 species heard across the 24 hours to 9:30 pm.

The Common Myna is new to these pages. The station, which has been listening for 68 days, recorded it for the first time today: two calls, the first at 8:21 am and the last at 8:24 am.

It is one of the most widespread birds around Canberra, and lives in the region year-round, yet the microphone had not picked it up until now. The common myna eats everything from grasshoppers and earthworms to fruit, nectar, roadkill and discarded human food.

The full day's list came to 13 species and 447 calls, well clear of the seven-day average of 330 calls. The busiest stretch was 5 am to 6 am, which produced 100 of them.

The Dawn Chorus

The chorus opened five minutes after first light, when a Pacific Koel called at 5:45 am, 22 minutes before sunrise. A Silvereye followed at 5:46 am, six minutes after first light, then a Pied Currawong at 5:58 am and a Eurasian Blackbird at 6:02 am, five minutes before the sun came up at 6:07 am. An Australian Magpie joined four minutes before sunrise, a Galah at 6:18 am, a Pied Butcherbird at 6:25 am, a Sulphur-crested Cockatoo at 6:44 am and a Yellow-rumped Thornbill at 7:12 am. Overnight, an Australian Magpie had called at 1:22 am and a Pacific Koel from 4:58 am.

The Regular

Sulphur-crested Cockatoo

The Sulphur-crested Cockatoo has been heard on 65 of the 68 days the station has been listening, and added 17 calls today. Its first came at 6:44 am, 36 minutes after sunrise, and its last at 4:41 pm.

It is a large white parrot with a bright yellow crest, black bill and grey legs.
