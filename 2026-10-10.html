Lead story

Grey fantail makes a brief morning appearance

Four calls in two minutes gave the grey fantail its moment in Saturday's chorus, while the magpie kept up its presence across the day.

The grey fantail was heard four times in the 24 hours to 9:30 pm, and all four calls came in a tight burst. The first arrived at 7:42 am, 74 minutes after sunrise, and the last came at 7:44 am. That was the bird's whole recorded day: two minutes of calling at the tail end of the chorus.

The station has heard the grey fantail on 9 of the 52 days it has been listening. In the Canberra region it is one of the most widespread birds, and it lives here year-round.

The grey fantail's call is an almost metallic 'cheek', given singly or repeated as a chattering. On Saturday morning the microphone picked up whatever shape those calls took, then the bird fell quiet for the rest of the day.

The Regular

Australian Magpie

The Australian Magpie was heard 26 times between 7:00 am and 7:46 pm, a span that stretched from just after sunrise into the evening. The station has recorded the species on most of the days it has been listening, 50 of 52, and it lives in the Canberra region year-round.

It is spring, and magpie breeding season. Magpies can recognise at least 100 different people and may spare those they have befriended when swooping.
