Lead story

Superb Lyrebird breaks into a quiet Friday

Two calls at 12:41 pm gave the Superb Lyrebird the lead today, while 15 species and 268 calls filled the 24 hours.

The Superb Lyrebird made its presence heard at 12:41 pm, with both of its calls in the 24 hours coming at that moment. It has been heard on 3 of the 65 days the station has been listening.

The bird is a moderately common one around Canberra and lives in the region year-round, yet midday records are scarce at this microphone. A single bird, heard and then gone, made for a short but striking appearance in an otherwise ordinary Friday.

One measure of the lyrebird's presence in the landscape: a single lyrebird can move and bury up to 200 tonnes of leaf litter and soil per hectare every year, helping reduce wildfire fuel.

The Regular

Eurasian Coot

The Eurasian Coot kept to its two calls, at 4:07 pm and again at 7:48 pm. It has now been heard on 39 of the 65 days the station has been listening.

The coot is one of the most widespread birds around Canberra and lives in the region year-round. For the record: it fights other waterbirds by charging its opponent and striking them with its long legs.

Did You Know?

  • Superb Lyrebird. Around 70–80% of a male's vocalisations are imitations of other species, mostly other birds but occasionally marsupials. [source]
  • Superb Lyrebird. The superb lyrebird's diet consists primarily of invertebrates such as earthworms and insects found on the forest floor. [source]
  • Eurasian Coot. The omnivorous coot eats small live prey, including the eggs of other waterbirds, as well as algae, vegetation, seeds and fruit. [source]
