Eastern Rosella makes five calls in a day

A rosella heard five times before midday set a new mark, while ten species and 113 calls filled the 24 hours to 9:30 pm.

The Eastern Rosella had its busiest day yet at the station. Five calls were heard in the 24 hours to 9:30 pm, all of them at 10:43 am, beating the previous best of 2 calls in a day.

It is one of the most widespread birds around Canberra and lives in the region year-round, yet the microphone has caught it on only 4 of the 57 days the station has been listening. A morning burst of five in one go, then, stood out.

The rosella is dressed for spring: white cheeks, a red head, a red upper breast, and a yellow lower breast that fades to pale green. In Australia the species usually breeds from August to February, nesting in hollow trees and nest boxes.

The Regular

Yellow-rumped Thornbill

The Yellow-rumped Thornbill was heard 13 times between 6:14 am and 6:45 pm, and has now featured on 57 of the 57 days the station has been listening. Its first call came seven minutes before sunrise.

The species mainly eats ants, beetles, bugs and lerps, and usually forages in small groups of 3 to 12 birds. Its dome-shaped nest has a false cup-shaped nest on top, while the real nest inside has a concealed entrance.
