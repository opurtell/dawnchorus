Oriole turns one minute into its biggest day

Eleven calls in a single minute were enough to make Friday 9 October the Olive-backed Oriole's busiest day at the microphone, in a 24 hours that brought 13 species and 234 calls.

The Olive-backed Oriole has had a day to itself. Between 6:46 am and 6:47 am today, the microphone registered 11 calls from the bird, all in that single minute. It was the busiest day yet for the species here; the previous best was 4 calls in a day.

The timing sits neatly in the morning. First light came at 6:03 am and sunrise at 6:29 am, and the oriole's first call arrived at 6:46 am, 17 minutes after sunrise. It was the sixth species to join the dawn chorus.

This is a summer migrant to the Canberra region, most often recorded in October, November and December, and a moderately common bird around Canberra. It is not bright in colour, with an olive back marked by small dark streaks. Across the whole 24 hours it was heard on 5 of the 51 days the station has been listening.

The Regular

Magpie-lark

The Magpie-lark gave two calls in the 24 hours, the first at 6:37 am, eight minutes after sunrise, and the last at 7:41 am. It has been heard on 37 of the 51 days the station has been listening, and it lives in the Canberra region year-round.

The species is one of only about 200 bird species worldwide that sing in duet, with partners singing half a second apart. Pairs sing duets together to defend their territory, and sing most vigorously at strangers rather than familiar neighbours.
