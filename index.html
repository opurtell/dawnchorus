Lead story

One lapwing call in the night

A single masked lapwing call after dark set the tone for a 24 hours in which the microphone in Canberra heard seven species and 29 calls.

The masked lapwing's whole contribution to the last 24 hours was one call, heard at 10:50 pm on Tuesday 13 October. It was the only bird recorded after dark, and it has now been heard on 8 of the 56 days the station has been listening.

Lapwings live in the Canberra region year-round and are among the most widespread birds around Canberra. They spend most of their time on the ground searching for worms and insects to eat.

To lure predators away, a lapwing may fiercely guard a non-existent nest or hop on a single leg as a distraction. Nesting happens on almost any open ground, including suburban parks, school ovals, supermarket carparks and flat rooftops.

The Regular

Silvereye

The Silvereye was heard six times between 6:13 am and 7:08 pm, opening with a call ten minutes before sunrise. It has turned up on 33 of the 56 days the station has been listening, and lives in the Canberra region year-round.

Silvereyes eat insects, spiders, berries, fruit and nectar, and visit bird tables for sugar water, bread, cooked meats and fat when winter food is scarce.
